# TEC — New Student Onboarding & World Discovery Experience
## Development Specification — Level 1

You are building a premium early-childhood educational platform called TEC (TriNova Education Centre).

This specification adds a complete first-time onboarding experience and an interactive interest-discovery system to the existing Level 1 product.

Do not treat this as a generic registration form. Build an emotionally welcoming, visually exceptional, interactive experience.

The child should feel that they are entering their own educational world.

---

## 1. The central companion: ANIS

The central companion's final working name is:

**Anis (أَنِيس)**

Anis is the child's personal learning companion.

The name is associated with companionship and friendliness.

Anis must have:
- A distinctive, memorable illustrated character.
- A warm and natural voice.
- A friendly, intelligent and encouraging personality.
- A consistent visual appearance throughout the application.
- The ability to address the child by their chosen name.
- Knowledge of the child's schedule and learning progress.
- The ability to introduce teachers, classrooms and discovery worlds.

Anis is not a generic chatbot.

Anis guides the child through their TEC experience, introduces activities, listens to the child and helps them understand what happens next.

Anis must never claim to be a human or encourage emotional dependence.

---

## 2. First-time onboarding

Build a beautifully illustrated, multi-stage onboarding experience.

### Stage 1 — Welcome

Anis appears and says:

"Assalamu alaikum! Welcome to TEC! My name is Anis. I'll be your learning companion. I'll help you meet your teachers, visit your classrooms and discover wonderful things."

Use natural speech, character animation and a friendly introduction.

### Stage 2 — The child's name

Anis asks:

"What should I call you?"

Allow the child or parent to enter the child's preferred name.

Store the preferred pronunciation if necessary.

Anis responds naturally using the name.

### Stage 3 — Introduction to TEC

Anis explains the compulsory learning areas in simple language:

- English
- Arabic
- Mathematics
- Computer and Technology
- Tarbiyah and Character
- World Discovery

Explain that every child receives these foundations.

Introduce the six teachers and show their illustrated characters.

### Stage 4 — The TEC week

Explain the weekly routine:

Monday–Friday: Learning days.

Saturday: Game Day.

Sunday: Exploration Day.

Explain this with illustrations and simple animations rather than long text.

### Stage 5 — Enter the World Explorer

Anis introduces the child's discovery worlds.

"Now, let me show you something exciting! There are so many wonderful worlds waiting for you."

Transition into a beautiful illustrated map containing approximately 24 exploration destinations.

Each destination must have:
- A unique illustration.
- A distinctive visual identity.
- A short spoken introduction from Anis.
- High-quality pictures or approved short visual material.
- A short interactive preview.
- A clear option to explore the world.
- A way to save it to the child's favourites.

### Example: Cars and Machines

Show an exciting but age-appropriate introduction:

"Have you ever seen a car so fast that it looks like it is flying along the road?"

Introduce different kinds of cars, engines, motorcycles, trains and machines through beautiful images and narration.

Offer the child the choice to explore further.

### Example: Chess Kingdom

Introduce the chessboard, pieces and the idea of making thoughtful decisions.

Use the story of a kingdom and its pieces to create curiosity.

### Example: History

Introduce life in ancient times, historical buildings, great inventions and different civilisations through age-appropriate stories.

Do not present historical inaccuracies or confuse imagination with fact.

Every world should have its own carefully designed introduction. Do not simply show a title and a generic description.

---

## 4. Choosing interests

Allow children to choose initial favourite worlds.

The child can:
- Add a world to My Worlds.
- Ask to see more.
- Explore a short preview.
- Return to the world map.
- Change their choices later.

Allow approximately three initial favourites as a gentle suggestion, not a compulsory limit.

Never make the child feel that choosing a world means permanently abandoning the others.

The child's selected interests are additional exploration pathways. They do not replace the compulsory curriculum.

A child who loves cars must still encounter animals, geography, science, literature, history and other fields.

The system should periodically offer new discoveries to encourage breadth.

Do not optimise the recommendations solely for clicks, watch time or repeated engagement.

---

## 5. Interactive discovery previews

Every destination should have a preview experience.

A preview may include:
- A beautiful photograph or illustration.
- A short narrated story.
- A short approved educational video.
- A simple interactive object.
- A sound or animation.
- One or two curiosity-provoking questions.

Do not embed YouTube, public social media or unrestricted external video platforms.

Use controlled, age-appropriate media stored in or delivered through TEC's approved content system.

Videos should be brief, purposeful and optional.

The child should not be required to watch videos to learn.

---

## 6. The recommendation system

The child may not know what they like yet.

If they do not choose, Anis should gently suggest one or two interesting destinations.

For example:

"Would you like to meet some wonderful animals?"

Or:

"Shall we travel into space and discover the planets?"

Never force a selection.

Never repeatedly ask the same question.

Never use manipulative countdowns, artificial scarcity or pressure.

Never create an algorithm that traps the child in a narrow interest.

Encourage balanced exposure to different fields.

---

## 7. The first learning day

After onboarding, Anis should introduce the child's first learning day.

Display:
- The child's name.
- The next activity.
- The relevant teacher.
- A short description.
- The classroom entrance.

Anis says:

"Wonderful! Your TEC journey is ready. Shall we meet your first teacher?"

Take the child into the classroom with a gentle transition.

The first lesson should be short, welcoming and age-appropriate.

---

## 8. Design requirements

The World Explorer must look exceptional.

Use:
- A beautiful illustrated landscape or world map.
- Distinctive destination illustrations.
- Subtle animations.
- Carefully selected typography.
- Large touch-friendly controls.
- Rich but harmonious colours.
- Consistent character artwork.
- Smooth navigation.
- Beautiful loading and transition states.

Do not make it resemble a conventional school dashboard.

Do not make it look like a generic AI-generated website.

Do not use random stock images for every card.

Do not overcrowd the interface.

The whole experience must feel like a professionally art-directed educational product.

---

## 9. Age and safety requirements

The target age is 2–4.

The onboarding must be supported by a parent or responsible adult.

Use short sentences, simple vocabulary, spoken instructions and large interactive elements.

Do not assume the child can read.

Do not collect unnecessary personal information.

Do not create public student profiles or social features.

Do not allow unrestricted browsing or external messaging.

Give parents control over media, audio, consent and learning schedules.

Respect limited screen-time recommendations.

Encourage offline play, physical exploration and real-world experiences.

---

## 10. Final product objective

The result should be a complete, working onboarding and discovery experience, not a static visual mockup.

Implement:
- The Anis companion.
- The welcome conversation.
- Name collection.
- Introduction to TEC.
- Teacher introductions.
- Weekly schedule introduction.
- The 24-world explorer.
- Interactive previews.
- Interest selection.
- My Worlds.
- The first classroom transition.

The experience must be integrated into the existing TEC Level 1 application.

Do not build Levels 2–8.

Do not add unnecessary social features, leaderboards or competitive ranking.

The guiding principle:

**TEC should open the doors of the world to a child, not decide which one door they must enter.**
